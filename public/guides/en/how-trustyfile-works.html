How TrustyFile analyses a document

Updated 11 November 2026

TrustyFile is a free tool that looks for the technical traces of forgery in a PDF. It does not read the document the way a person does: it examines how the file was made, then weighs the evidence it finds. This page describes the method, and above all its limits.

In short

  • TrustyFile examines a PDF with more than a dozen modules: metadata, revisions, fonts, covered or invisible text, images, the producing software's fingerprint, arithmetic, company identifiers and 2D-Doc codes.
  • Every anomaly becomes a weighted piece of evidence; together they give a score from 0 to 100 and a risk level, with an explanation for each finding.
  • A document with nothing to examine, such as a flattened scan, is marked "unverifiable" instead of receiving a good score by default.
  • A high score means few signs of manipulation, not certain authenticity; only a valid 2D-Doc signature proves the data it covers is genuine.

What gets examined

The uploaded file goes through more than a dozen detection modules, each one specialised in one kind of trace:

  • Metadata: producing software, creation and modification dates, a pass through an online converter.
  • PDF structure: incremental revisions, deleted objects still present, JavaScript, digital signatures.
  • Producer fingerprint: the way the file is written, byte by byte, which gives away a forged or stripped Producer field.
  • Text: a font change mid-line, text covered by a rectangle, invisible text that contradicts what is shown.
  • Images: pasted blocks, resolution breaks, EXIF metadata, error level analysis (ELA) on edited areas.
  • Content: impossible dates, inconsistent amounts, pay slip arithmetic, e-invoice totals compared with their XML.
  • Identifiers: SIREN, SIRET, VAT and IBAN check digits, the company's existence in the French INSEE register.
  • Official codes: 2D-Doc decoded and its signature verified, the MRZ of digital identity documents.

Weighed evidence, not a checklist

Every anomaly becomes a piece of evidence, weighted by its strength: a font changed in the middle of a word weighs more than a pass through an online compressor. The evidence is combined into a score from 0 to 100 and a risk level (low, medium, high or critical).

The type of document matters too. TrustyFile recognises an invoice, a pay slip, an identity document, and does not read a clue the same way everywhere: what is normal for a kind of document must not count against it.

The results page explains each finding in one sentence and outlines, on the document preview, the area it concerns.

Knowing when to say "unverifiable"

A flattened scan or a photo has no text layer, no fonts and no history: most checks have nothing to examine. Finding no anomaly then proves nothing, and TrustyFile shows "unverifiable", with no score, rather than a good score by default. Asking for the original PDF makes verification possible.

The one check that proves: the 2D-Doc signature

All the clues above make forgery more or less likely. A cryptographic signature is different in kind: when a document carries a 2D-Doc code, TrustyFile verifies its ECDSA signature offline, with the certificates published by the French ANTS. A valid signature establishes that the data in the code is the issuer's. The 2D-Doc guide details what that proves, and what it does not.

What TrustyFile cannot do

  • Certify that a document is genuine: a high score means few signs of manipulation, nothing more.
  • Reliably detect a forgery built from scratch with the original software: it may leave no editing trace at all.
  • Tell who owns a bank account, or whether the person sending a document is who they claim to be.
  • Replace checking with the issuer: SVAIR for a French tax notice, the URSSAF security code for a vigilance certificate, a call to the supplier for a change of bank details.

What happens to your documents

The file is analysed, then deleted. The results page is kept in memory only, and disappears after fifteen minutes without being viewed, one hour at most. Only the file's hash and the company identifiers (SIRET, VAT) may be sent to outside services (VirusTotal, INSEE, VIES) to check them.

The analysis on the site is free, with no sign-up. It is also available through the API, with a key: POST /api/v1/analyze returns the same JSON report as the results page.

Frequently asked questions

Is TrustyFile reliable?
TrustyFile finds the technical traces of editing and explains each one. It certifies nothing: a high score means few signs of manipulation, a low score deserves a manual check, and a document with no usable signal is marked unverifiable. A valid 2D-Doc signature is the only result that proves the data it covers is genuine.
Does TrustyFile keep my documents?
No. The file is analysed, then deleted; the result stays in memory for fifteen minutes without being viewed, one hour at most.
Can TrustyFile be used through an API?
Yes, with an API key: ask for one at contact@trustyfile.com. POST /api/v1/analyze takes a PDF and returns the analysis report as JSON, the same as the results-page download. Each key has a monthly quota of analyses. The documentation is at trustyfile.com/api/docs.
