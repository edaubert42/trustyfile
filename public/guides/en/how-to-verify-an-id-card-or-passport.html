How to verify an ID card or passport

Updated 11 November 2026

In a rental application, an account opening or a sale, what you receive is almost always a copy of the ID, as a photo or a PDF. A copy does not let you check the physical security features (hologram, inks, laser engraving). Three things remain to check: the MRZ, the consistency of the data and the origin of the file.

In short

  • On a copy of an ID document, the most useful check is the MRZ: its check digits must be right and its data identical to what is printed on the document.
  • The French ID card comes in two formats: the old one (1988-2021) has a 2-line MRZ of 36 characters, the new card-size one 3 lines of 30.
  • Right check digits do not prove authenticity, since the algorithm is public; wrong check digits on a digital document are a strong signal.
  • To protect your own ID, add a watermark with the French state's free FiligraneFacile service before sending it.

Read and check the MRZ

The MRZ is the band of characters ending in chevrons (<<<), at the bottom of a passport's photo page or on the back of an ID card. It repeats the document type, country, name, number, date of birth, sex and expiry date, and several fields are followed by a check digit (ICAO 9303).

Copy the lines into our MRZ checker: it recognises the format (passport, new card, old French card) and checks every digit. On a photo, re-read the ambiguous characters: 0 and O, 8 and B, 1 and I.

Compare the MRZ with the printed data

This is the most telling check. A forger who changes the name or date of birth in the visible part often forgets to redo the MRZ, or redoes it without recomputing the check digits. Make sure these match:

  • surname and given names (without accents in the MRZ);
  • the date of birth, written YYMMDD in the MRZ;
  • the document number;
  • the expiry date, except on the old French card, whose MRZ has none.

Check the validity dates

An adult passport is valid for 10 years, and so is the new French ID card. Beware of the old card: if it was issued to an adult between 2 January 2004 and 31 December 2013, it stays valid for 15 years, even though the printed date says 10. A card "expired" for less than five years is therefore not necessarily fake.

Look at the file itself

When the copy is a PDF, its origin shows in its metadata and structure: a file run through an image or PDF editor, a pasted ID photo whose resolution differs from the rest, a font that changes on the name or the date, a revision added afterwards. The tells are covered in our guide to modified PDFs.

Protect your own ID

A copy sent for a rental can be reused for identity theft. The French state's free FiligraneFacile service (filigrane.beta.gouv.fr) adds a watermark naming who the document is for and why, for example "For a rental application only".

Frequently asked questions

How do I verify an ID card from a copy?
Check the MRZ check digits, compare the MRZ with the printed name, date of birth and number, check the validity date, then the file's origin if it is a PDF.
Is an expired French ID card necessarily invalid?
Not always. A card issued to an adult between 2 January 2004 and 31 December 2013 stays valid for 15 years, even though the printed date says 10.
Does a valid MRZ prove the ID is genuine?
No. The check-digit algorithm is public. A valid MRZ only proves it is internally consistent; it must also match the printed data.
